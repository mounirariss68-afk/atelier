The Answer, Up Front
Core Hypothesis

Our focus is singular: EBITDA. Not AI for AI's sake — not transformation theatre. Every recommendation in this assessment is evaluated against one question: does it move the EBITDA needle, and how fast?

The fastest path to EBITDA impact in Doğan Mining is not boiling the ocean with building new AI platforms — it is closing the gap between how your processing assets perform today and what they can deliver at their full potential. We believe five focused bets are worth exploring right now.

Peers doing exactly this are already seeing 2–5% throughput gains, 70% reductions in unplanned downtime, and concentrate recovery improvements of 3–5 percentage points — using the data that is already being generated inside the plant today.

Internal value logic · data likely existsAssessment
Recovery rates in the flotation circuit are running 2–5% below the engineering ceiling this plant was designed to deliver — every percentage point represents direct concentrate revenue lost per tonne processed.
Unplanned downtime on mills, crushers, and pumps is typically the single highest driver of variable OPEX in a processing operation — each stoppage costs not just repair time but lost throughput that cannot be recovered.
Throughput and energy efficiency vary significantly with ore hardness, yet most operations run fixed settings calibrated for average ore — not the actual feed coming through at any given hour.
Final concentrate grade assay results take 4–8 hours to return from the laboratory — during that window, process decisions are made blind, meaning both under-recovery and off-spec production go undetected for hours.
Suboptimal ore blending driven by gut feel or simple rules rather than grade data results in inconsistent mill feed — forcing the processing circuit to operate on wrong settings and degrading recovery across the entire flowsheet.
Reagent dosing decisions are made manually at fixed intervals, while feed grade changes continuously in real time — this systematic lag creates predictable and correctable recovery losses that an AI control layer can close.
Vibration, temperature, and current draw data is commonly captured on critical rotating equipment, but rarely analysed predictively — failures develop over days or weeks before they become visible to operators.
Mill power draw, feed rate, and particle size data is already captured in SCADA at most operations, but rarely used for real-time decisions — meaning there is an untapped optimisation signal in existing systems today.
A predictive model using real-time process sensor data can estimate concentrate grade continuously — giving operators a live quality signal to act on rather than waiting for lab confirmation, with no new measurement hardware required.
Stockpile grade tracking and draw-down history are typically logged in operations systems without being used analytically — making this a data-ready use case that can be built on existing records with no new infrastructure.
The data needed — pH, density, reagent flow, and lab assay results — is almost certainly already being captured by SCADA, requiring no new hardware investment to start.
A targeted ML model on 2–3 critical assets at one priority site can demonstrate measurable downtime reduction within 90 days, without requiring a site-wide sensor deployment programme first.
