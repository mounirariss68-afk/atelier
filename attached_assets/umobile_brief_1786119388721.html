Sales Meeting Brief · 7 August 2026

U Mobile — Lau Chee Fook

Head of Base Marketing / Digital Consumer Management · cheefook.lau@u.com.my · Kuala Lumpur

The 30-second version. U Mobile is the #4-by-subs, #3-by-revenue Malaysian telco that just pulled off the biggest upset in the market: it won the mandate to build Malaysia's second 5G network, built it three months ahead of schedule, and on 1 July 2026 cut ties with DNB entirely. It now owns its own nationwide network and is in full land-grab mode — while carrying a RM722m statutory loss, ~RM2bn negative equity and a multi-billion capex programme.

Your contact runs base monetisation — upsell, cross-sell, retention, plan migration on ~9.6m subscribers. That is the single most outbound-heavy function in the company, and it is the exact spot where U Mobile has zero public AI capability today. Their AI story is all network and ecosystem. Commercial AI is white space.

The strategic prize they're chasing. Malaysia's mobile market is flat at ~50m lines, but the mix is shifting hard: postpaid grew +2.1m to 16.9m (2023→2025) while prepaid fell >2.4m. Industry service revenue is forecast +2–3% in 2026–27, the strongest since 2013.

U Mobile is historically prepaid-skewed and a value-brand. It is now using network quality to move upmarket — the April 2026 ULTRA5G relaunch, free 15GB roaming across 60+ destinations, Priority Passes, ULTRA Family 128. Prepaid→postpaid migration on the existing base is the highest-value campaign in the company, and it is Lau Chee Fook's job. That is your wedge.

4 — Their AI strategy (and the hole in it)

U Mobile's AI posture is network-led and ecosystem-led, not commercially-led. Almost everything public sits in two buckets: AI embedded in the RAN/core via Huawei and ZTE, and the EIP partner ecosystem. Their only disclosed internal AI deployment is an inbound contact centre programme on AWS.

What they have Real

  • AWS GenAI contact centre — PoC Jul 2024, announced Dec 2024, deployed from Q1 2025. Bedrock + SageMaker. Post Call Analytics and Live Call Agent Assist. Claimed first ASEAN carrier to do it. Owned by CIO Neil Tomkinson. Inbound only. No published metrics, ever.
  • Cellusys network scam firewall — behavioural/signalling anomaly detection. 390m+ scam attempts blocked at network level. Malaysia's first network-level scam protection. Heavily marketed.
  • EIP Hub — launched 6 Aug 2026, 15 partners (AWS, Huawei, Palo Alto, Qualcomm anchor). A neutral 5G-A/AI sandbox. Ministerial and MCMC backing.

What they don't White space

  • Nothing in sales, telesales, outbound, lead gen, churn prediction, next-best-offer or CVM. Zero public disclosure. Searched hard.
  • No consumer-facing AI assistant. No U Mobile equivalent of Maxis's Mia or CelcomDigi's SPARK AI. Nothing AI in the MyUMobile app.
  • No AI data centre, no GPU investment. (TM has sovereign GPU-as-a-Service on Nvidia; YTL runs a US$4.3bn Nvidia AI DC in Johor. U Mobile isn't in that race.)
  • No quantified ROI from any AI initiative and no disclosed AI capex figure, ever.
  • No Microsoft, Google, Nvidia, Salesforce, Amdocs or Netcracker relationships. Cloud is AWS-only.

The competitive shame angle — use it carefully. CelcomDigi publishes hard AI numbers: 25% reduction in average resolution time, 37% of inquiries resolved via digital self-service, an AI-as-a-Service product for SMEs, all built in-house. Maxis has a branded genAI concierge and an AI fraud firewall. On network AI, U Mobile is genuinely competitive — AI-native core, Level 4 autonomy targets with Huawei, ZTE AIR Core pilots. On commercial and customer-facing AI it is visibly last. Lau Chee Fook almost certainly knows this. Don't rub his nose in it — frame it as "you're first-mover on the revenue side, where they're not."

6 — Objections to pre-empt

"We already do AI with AWS."

Their AWS work is inbound contact centre assist — Post Call Analytics and Live Agent Assist. It helps human agents on calls customers initiate. It does not place calls, does not run campaigns, and has never had a published outcome metric. Frame Acrely as the outbound counterpart to an inbound investment they already made, not a replacement for it. Extending Bedrock into outbound is an 18-month internal build with no owner — there's no CDO and no AI leader.

Language — likely the hardest technical objection

Malaysia is Bahasa Malaysia, English, Mandarin/Cantonese/Hokkien, Tamil — and in practice heavy code-switching within a single sentence ("Manglish"). U Mobile's base skews value, youth and migrant-worker segments, which makes this harder, not easier. Have a concrete answer ready — ideally a live demo in Bahasa with English code-switching. Note Maxis specifically publicised LLM training supporting Bahasa Melayu with AWS; they will benchmark you against that.

Scam and regulatory sensitivity Handle with care

This is the sharpest risk in the room and you should raise it before he does. U Mobile has built a brand pillar out of blocking scam calls — 390m+ blocked, "Malaysia's first telco with network-level scam protection," a Palo Alto SECaaS MoU, ministerial praise. Malaysia is in the middle of a national scam-call crackdown, and MCMC is directly involved with U Mobile.

An AI voice calling their customers is, on its face, in tension with all of that. Get ahead of it: disclosure and identification design, consent and DNC handling, opt-out, call recording and audit trail, PDPA compliance, human escalation paths. Turn the objection into a differentiator — "you're the telco that already knows what a bad outbound call sounds like; you should be the one setting the standard for a good AI one."

Data sovereignty Your advantage

Malaysia's AI Governance Bill is expected to be tabled in 2026 — NAIO's public consultation closed 31 July 2026, six days ago. It proposes a central AI authority, three risk tiers, mandatory incident reporting including near-misses, and a responsible-data-governance principle. Add PDPA, a royally-chaired Malaysian-majority owner, a "Made by Malaysia" 13MP agenda (RM611bn), and a company whose foreign-ownership cap is a licence condition. Acrely's on-prem deployment option is a genuinely strong card here — play it early and hard.

Budget and timing

Capital is going into the network. Every ringgit is contested. But the flip side: they just finished the expensive part of the build, they're now trying to monetise it, and CGS expects them to be at scale by ~2028. Propose a small, ring-fenced, single-campaign pilot with a defined success metric — something Lau Chee Fook can approve or get approved without a board paper. Do not propose a platform.

"Where does prepaid-to-postpaid migration sit in your KPIs this year?"The market mix shift is the defining dynamic and U Mobile is prepaid-heavy. If this is his number, you've found the deal.

"Given the scam-protection brand position, what would an AI-initiated call need to look like for you to be comfortable putting it in front of customers?"Raising this yourself buys enormous credibility and gets you their real compliance bar before you build to the wrong one.

8 — Things to know but not say

  • The 5G award is politically contested. "Crony-poly" allegations, an opaque tender, a ruling-coalition MP disciplined for criticising it, and now the King's daughter as Chairman. Do not go near this.
  • Their coverage numbers are flattering. 82.9% CoPA measures sites built; Opensignal measured 5G availability at ~60%, and a SoyaCincau road test connected to ULTRA5G at 5 of 19 KL locations. Since the DNB fallback is now gone, this matters. Don't raise it — but don't repeat their coverage claims back to them as though they're gospel either.
  • The IPO is stalled. Mooted Jan 2025 at RM11–12bn; nothing has happened, and no 2026 press release mentions it. The gap versus the RM7.5bn Temasek transaction valuation is wide. Probably 2027–28 at earliest. Don't ask about it.
  • Huawei/ZTE dependency drew Western security pushback. Irrelevant to you and a sore subject.
  • No FY2025 accounts are public. FY2024 is the latest hard data. Don't cite FY25 numbers — you'd be citing guesses.
