# Cognirise — Alliance Partner Evaluation Framework

_Version 2.0 · July 2026 · Private & Confidential_

A repeatable methodology for evaluating, tiering, structuring and contracting **organizational** alliance partners (consultancies, technology vendors, delivery firms, platform providers and joint-venture entities). It takes a candidate from first contact to a signed agreement and into ongoing management, in a consistent and defensible way.

**Scope note.** This framework covers partnerships **between Cognirise and other organizations only**. Arrangements with individuals (personal hires, individual advisors or originators) are out of scope and are handled under Cognirise's people/HR and non-equity-partner processes, not here.

**How to use it.** Work top to bottom. Stages 1–3 decide _whether_ and _as what_ to partner; Stages 4–6 decide _how_ to structure and contract; Stages 7–9 keep it de-risked, legal, phased and well-managed over time. Each stage ends with a decision recorded on the one-page worksheet in the Appendix. The prompts in the final section let you run the whole thing with Claude.

---

## 1. Guiding principles

These anchor every decision and break ties when trade-offs conflict.

- **Fit first.** The primary selection criterion is fit of service offering and industry coverage with Cognirise — not brand nationality or size. A referenceable enterprise track record matters more than prestige.
- **Complementary, not competing.** Build a portfolio of partners that fill distinct gaps and do not cannibalise each other or Cognirise's own differentiation.
- **Own the client, own the brand.** On Cognirise-originated work, Cognirise holds the client relationship, the commercial brand and (wherever possible) the foreground IP; partners plug in beneath that.
- **Pay for value delivered, not value assumed.** Reward access, delivery and origination that actually materialise; gate the rest behind evidence and milestones.
- **Protect the downside.** Every partnership is structured so Cognirise is protected legally, commercially and reputationally even in a dispute, underperformance or change-of-control scenario.
- **Evidence over assertion.** Claims about capability, references and reach are verified, not taken at face value.

## 2. Partner archetypes (the role in the portfolio)

Classify every candidate into one primary archetype. The archetype drives the deal structure (Stage 4) and the direction of the core clauses (who licenses whom, who keeps IP, who primes).

| Archetype | What they provide | Cognirise's typical role |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference / brand partner** | Premium references, methods and IP to represent or resell in-region | Regional front line; represents/resells their brand |
| **Delivery / engineering backbone** | Scaled, cost-competitive delivery capacity | Prime; owns client & brand; subcontracts them |
| **Platform / capability partner** | A proprietary platform plus the engineers to run it, for a specific service line | Joint go-to-market; originate and (sometimes) prime |
| **Niche / specialist subcontractor** | Specialist build or vertical capability for defined scope | Prime; subcontracts them for that scope |
| **Market-access joint venture (entity)** | Local ownership, relationships and licence-to-win in a target market, via a jointly-owned company | Co-owner; retains operational control |
| **Competitor — monitor only** | Overlapping offering, thinner references | None; watch, do not partner |

## 3. Stage 1 — Strategic-fit assessment (scored)

Score each dimension 1–5 (5 = strong). Weights reflect Cognirise's thesis; change them only with a documented reason. Weights sum to 100%.

| # | Dimension | What "strong" looks like | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Service alignment** | Their offerings map directly onto a Cognirise service line | 20% |
| 2 | **Industry / sector coverage** | Depth where regional demand concentrates (FS, mfg, life sciences, E&C, telecom, government) | 15% |
| 3 | **Delivery economics** | Improves our price competitiveness (nearshore/offshore mix, favourable rates) | 15% |
| 4 | **References that travel** | Marquee, verifiable logos that regional buyers screen for | 10% |
| 5 | **Complementary footprint** | Little/no presence in our target region → additive, no channel conflict | 10% |
| 6 | **AI-differentiation fit** | Reinforces (or at least doesn't dilute) our AI-native positioning | 10% |
| 7 | **Relationship, cultural & operating-model fit** | Warm, senior, trust-based; compatible delivery methodology and working style | 10% |
| 8 | **Portfolio complementarity** | Fills a distinct gap; no overlap/conflict with current partners | 5% |
| 9 | **Region relevance** | Genuine reach into our target geographies | 5% |

**Decision gate:** weighted score ≥ 3.5 → proceed to due diligence. 2.5–3.5 → proceed only in a limited/monitored role. < 2.5 → decline or monitor-only. Record the two lowest-scoring dimensions as watch-outs carried into Stage 7.

> The weights and thresholds are calibrated heuristics, not precise instruments — treat a marginal score as a prompt for judgement, not an automatic verdict.

---

## 4. Stage 2 — Organizational due diligence (pass / conditional / fail)

Strategic fit answers "should we _want_ this partner." Due diligence answers "can we _rely_ on them and does associating with them create risk." These are gates, not scores. This stage is a deliberate correction to the common blind spot of choosing partners on selling appeal alone.

- **Legal existence & good standing** — verified entity, licences, registrations; authority to contract in the relevant jurisdictions.
- **Financial viability & backing** — solvency, funding/ownership, and the ability to sustain delivery through the term (critical where they are a delivery backbone we depend on).
- **Delivery capability & quality track record** — independent reference checks on _them_ (not just their marketing logos): real client outcomes, attrition, on-time/on-quality history.
- **Information security & data protection posture** — certifications, controls and willingness to sign a DPA where they touch client data.
- **Ownership transparency & change-of-control risk** — who ultimately owns/controls them, and what happens (to exclusivity, IP, our clients) if they are acquired — potentially by a competitor.
- **Litigation, sanctions & adverse media** — screening of the entity and its principals.
- **Insurance & liability capacity** — adequate cover to stand behind indemnities and delivery obligations.

**Output:** pass / conditional (with remediation) / fail. A fail here overrides a strong fit score.

---

## 5. Stage 3 — Role & tier decision

Confirm the archetype (Section 2) and place the partner in a portfolio tier:

- **Tier 1 — Strategic:** central to the offering (reference brand, delivery backbone, market-access JV). Full agreement, deep terms, active governance.
- **Tier 2 — Capability / on-demand:** used per-opportunity for a specific service line or platform. Scoped alliance MoU.
- **Tier 3 — Tactical / monitor:** occasional subcontract, or a competitor to watch. Light or no contract.

Explicitly confirm **no conflict** with existing partners (e.g., a delivery backbone must not compete with the reference brand's delivery; a platform partner must not undercut Cognirise's own tooling service line).

---

## 6. Stage 4 — Deal-structure selection

Choose the structure using four questions; the answers set the contract type and the direction of the core clauses.

1. **Who originates the client?** (Cognirise / Partner / either)
2. **Who owns the client relationship & brand?** (usually Cognirise on our-originated work)
3. **Who delivers?** (Partner / Cognirise / blended)
4. **Who primes — holds the contract & the risk?** (Cognirise / Partner / case-by-case)

| Structure | When to use |
|---|---|
| **Reseller / representation MoU** | We represent or resell the partner's brand, IP and services in-region |
| **Delivery-subcontract MoU** | Partner is our engineering/delivery backbone; we prime and own the client |
| **Scoped alliance MoU (joint GTM)** | Joint selling of a specific platform/service line; prime varies per deal |
| **Equity joint venture (entity)** | We need local ownership/relationships/licence-to-win a market, via a jointly-owned company |

**Binding posture.** An MoU can be deliberately non-binding, fully binding, or (recommended default) **binding operational core** — pricing, exclusivity, IP, confidentiality, non-circumvention and fees are binding, while pipeline/pursuit ambitions stay aspirational. Make it **mutual and balanced** where the partner is a peer, and firmer in our favour where they are a supplier to us. State the posture explicitly; do not leave it ambiguous.

## 7. Stage 5 — Commercial-model toolkit

Assemble compensation from these building blocks. Standardise the definitions; treat the numbers as **illustrative default parameters to be set per deal**, not market benchmarks.

### Standard definitions (use consistently)
- **Project / gross margin:** realised gross margin _after client acceptance_ = fees invoiced and collected, minus all direct delivery costs (subcontractors, partner rates, delivery time), excluding overhead/G&A. Never pay on unrealised, disputed or uncollected fees.
- **Fee base (alliance deals):** the counterparty's services revenue actually invoiced and collected, excluding pure software-licence/pass-through costs and taxes.
- **Material (sales) contribution:** active, substantive involvement in winning the work beyond a mere introduction (leading or shaping the proposal, solutioning, pricing, negotiation, close).

### Building blocks (with illustrative defaults — configure per deal)
- **Rate card:** either _price-to-Cognirise_ (simple, no open book) or _transparent cost-plus_ (open book, e.g. cost + an agreed margin). Add a **most-favoured-customer** clause (optionally region-scoped), a fixed period, and a capped annual increase.
- **Profit / margin share** for a delivery or joint partner — e.g. a defined % of gross margin, or a **split model** (an origination component + a sales-participation component that are additive).
- **Referral fee** — a % of the fee base for a pure referral.
- **Sales-participation fee** — an additional % where the originator actively participates in or leads the sale.
- **Origination tail** — a smaller % for a fixed period (commonly seen in the 12–24 month range) on repeat work at an originated account where the originator no longer actively contributes; steps up to the full referral + participation if they do.
- **Prime / subcontract economics** — where Cognirise primes, it keeps its prime margin and pays the partner for delivery; no separate fee flows back to us.
- **Equity + conversion (JV / staged deals)** — a variable-first arrangement converting to a fuller structure on a hard, measurable milestone (e.g. a defined bookings level at a target realised margin), with equity valued and agreed at conversion.

### Discipline rules
- **No double-counting.** A single opportunity earns _either_ the referral + participation combination _or_ the origination tail — not both.
- **Margin-stack check (blind-spot guard).** Before committing, model the fully-loaded economics: after the partner's rates plus any share/fee, can Cognirise still price competitively _and_ retain an acceptable margin? Reject structures that leave us uncompetitive or margin-thin.
- **Pay on realisation.** Percentage compensation is calculated on collected, accepted amounts.

## 8. Stage 6 — Protective-terms checklist

Include, or consciously waive with a reason, each of the following.

- **Exclusivity / first right of refusal** — scoped and time-boxed (e.g. a fixed, extendable registration period), never blanket market exclusivity. Trigger via a **deal-registration** mechanism to prevent the same opportunity being shopped to multiple partners.
- **Account protection & non-circumvention** — partner engages our originated clients only through us; explicit non-circumvention with a defined tail; liquidated-damages hook where warranted.
- **Non-solicitation of personnel** — mutual, for a defined period.
- **IP ownership** — foreground deliverables to Cognirise/client on our-originated work; partner keeps background IP with a licence to us. Reversed for reseller deals (partner keeps its IP; we get a licence to sell).
- **IP non-replication (platform partners)** — a covenant not to copy the platform, **anchored to misuse of confidential information**, with explicit carve-outs for **independent development**, **residual knowledge** and **industry-wide practice**, so it cannot be used to shut down Cognirise's own service lines.
- **Delivery, quality & remedies** — where we prime: replacement of underperformers at cost, step-in / re-sourcing rights; keep heavy SLA/KPI schedules out of an MoU unless the partner is a pure supplier.
- **Confidentiality** — mutual; multi-year survival; terms of the agreement confidential.
- **Indemnities & liability** — mutual; each indemnifies for its own IP infringement and delivery; consequential loss excluded; caps deferred to the definitive agreement / SOW.
- **Change-of-control** — rights to review, renegotiate or exit if the partner is acquired, especially by a competitor.
- **Term / termination / survival** — defined term; notice proportional to our dependency; survival of confidentiality, IP, accrued fees, in-flight deals and any origination tail.
- **Governance** — named relationship leads; review cadence (more frequent for suppliers, quarterly for peers); escalation path.
- **Branding** — reciprocal reference rights for peers; white-label (partner delivers under our brand) where they are our subcontractor.

## 9. Stage 7 — Compliance & legal gates (hard pass/fail)

No agreement signs until these are cleared.

- **Anti-bribery / anti-corruption (FCPA / UK Bribery Act / local law).** Highest risk when a partner's value is _access to government or state-owned entities_ (their staff can be treated as officials). Never pay a fee contingent on a specific government/SOE award or an official's action. Require bona-fide documented services, market-rate compensation, PEP / beneficial-ownership / adverse-media due diligence, anti-corruption representations & warranties, a binding anti-bribery policy, and audit rights.
- **Anti-concealment / fronting.** Where local-ownership rules apply, a local partner's stake must be genuinely funded and its role real — never a nominee/front. Decoupling _control_ from _equity_ via a shareholders' agreement is legitimate; a sham is not.
- **Commercial-agency law.** Avoid inadvertently creating a hard-to-exit registered agency; prefer clean equity/subcontract structures with defined exit.
- **Sanctions & export control.** Screen counterparties; for defense/industrials or sovereign work, add export-control / end-user / end-use and national-security compliance.
- **Data protection & security.** DPA and minimum security standards wherever the partner touches client data.
- **Local regulatory readiness.** Where relevant: market-entry licence, regional-HQ requirements to win government contracts, localisation/nationalisation employment thresholds, and procurement preferences favouring local ownership.
- **Accuracy / evenhandedness.** Never overstate a partner's reach or references in our own materials; every claim must be verifiable.

> The compliance items above are general principles, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice. Confirm each with qualified counsel in the relevant market before signing.

## 10. Stage 8 — Risk register & standard watch-outs

Carry the two lowest fit scores (Stage 1) and any due-diligence conditions (Stage 2), plus any of these, into a short register with a mitigation for each.

- **Unverified claims** — capability, references or reach not corroborated → milestone-gate; require evidence.
- **Concentration / over-dependence** — too much of our delivery or revenue riding on one partner → cap exposure; keep an alternative.
- **Contribution / value asymmetry** — we bring capital, IP and delivery against unproven partner intangibles → stage the commitment; prove before you pay.
- **Change-of-control / acquisition by a competitor** → change-of-control rights; portable IP and client contracts.
- **Partner viability failure mid-delivery** → financial diligence up front; step-in and continuity plans.
- **Channel conflict / competitive overlap** — partner competes with us or another partner → scope limits, carve-outs, data-leakage controls.
- **Market / revenue concentration** — over-reliance on one buyer segment → diversify from the outset.
- **Exit / deadlock** — especially in 50/50 structures → reserved matters, casting vote, clear deadlock and exit terms.

---

## 11. Stage 9 — Phasing, conversion & lifecycle management

**Phase the commitment** — do not front-load equity or hard commitments before value is proven:

1. **Verify** — evidence of the partner's claimed value (references, capability proof, named opportunities); complete due-diligence and compliance screening.
2. **Pilot** — a light, low-commitment arrangement (a referral/BD deal or a first joint pursuit) with clear success milestones.
3. **Convert** — on hitting the milestone, upgrade to the full structure (exclusivity, equity, deeper terms), documented via an addendum.

**Manage the lifecycle** (the stage most partner processes forget):

- A **partner scorecard** tracking bookings, delivery quality, margin, responsiveness and compliance against agreed targets.
- Regular **business reviews** (cadence per tier) with a documented action log.
- Defined **renewal / step-up / wind-down triggers**, so under-performing or misaligned partnerships are actively de-escalated rather than drifting.

## 13. Methodology notes & limitations

- **Heuristic weights.** The Stage 1 weights and thresholds are decision aids calibrated to current strategy; recalibrate as the strategy evolves. They structure judgement; they do not replace it.
- **Illustrative parameters.** All commercial percentages, periods and thresholds in Stage 5 are configurable defaults, not external market benchmarks. Where a "typical range" is cited it is a general observation to be validated per deal and market, not a guarantee.
- **Compliance is general.** Stage 7 states principles, not legal advice; validate against qualified counsel in each jurisdiction.
- **Evidence standard.** Wherever the framework relies on a partner's own claims, those claims must be independently corroborated before they influence tier, structure or economics.
- **Two-sided fairness.** For peer partnerships, mutuality is a design goal: one-sided terms tend to fail at negotiation and strain the relationship in delivery.

## 14. Running the framework — prompts and skill

There is **no dedicated, installed skill** that runs this partner-evaluation framework today. (The installed account-research workflow targets _prospective clients_, not _alliance partners_, and should not be substituted for it.) Until such a skill is created, use the ready-to-run prompts below with Claude / Cowork. An optional skill definition is included at the end if you wish to package this as a reusable skill.

### 14.1 Prompt — full partner evaluation

```
You are an alliance-partnership strategist and corporate-development lead for Cognirise.
Run the Cognirise Alliance Partner Evaluation Framework (organizations only) on the
partner below. If any input is missing, research it from public sources and label each
finding Evidenced / Inferred / Unverified. Never fabricate facts, references or numbers.

PARTNER: <name / website>
CONTEXT WE HAVE: <how they came to us, any prior discussion, target region, service line>

Produce, in order:
1. Archetype & proposed tier (with reasoning).
2. Stage 1 fit scorecard: score all nine dimensions 1–5, apply the weights, give the
   weighted total and the proceed/limited/decline verdict; name the two weakest dimensions.
3. Stage 2 organizational due diligence: pass / conditional / fail across legal standing,
   financial viability, delivery/quality track record, security & data posture, ownership &
   change-of-control, litigation/sanctions/adverse media, insurance — flag gaps to verify.
4. Stage 4 deal-structure recommendation (answer the four origination/ownership/delivery/
   prime questions, then pick the structure and binding posture).
5. Stage 5 commercial model: the building blocks and suggested parameters, plus a
   margin-stack check that our economics remain competitive and profitable.
6. Stage 6 protective terms to include, and Stage 7 compliance gates with any red flags
   (especially anti-bribery where the partner touches government/state-owned buyers).
7. Stage 8 risk register (top 5 risks + mitigations).
8. Stage 9 phasing: what to do now (verify / pilot / convert) and the milestone to convert.
9. The Stage 12 recommendation template, filled in, ending with Go / No-go / Go-with-conditions.

Do not name or compare other Cognirise partners. Flag every assumption. End with the
specific evidence you would need to raise confidence.
```
